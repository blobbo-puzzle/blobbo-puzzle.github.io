# Level audit

Generated by `npm run audit:levels`. All 48 built-in maps have a legal winning route: 20 campaign rooms and 26 practice/experimental rooms, and 2 challenge rooms. Generator seeds 0–999 also pass. This proves the shipped layouts are solvable under this engine; arbitrary edited rooms still need playtesting.

## What was checked

- Validate each layout and replay every stored action through the actual engine. Reject death, blocked moves, incomplete collection, or an unfinished exit. Confirm replay leaves the original map unchanged.
- Remove exact-state cycles and try deleting contiguous chunks from campaign routes. Replay every accepted shortcut. These witnesses are **upper bounds**, not globally shortest solutions; the audit does not rule out other, much shorter approaches.
- The separate test suite exercises irreversible mistakes, remote locks, shared supports, tool exchanges, and specific object-role changes. Historical capped searches in map metadata are not treated as proofs of impossibility.
- Keep all geometry and winning references intact. Reorder rooms into four bands; migrate old position-based saves to stable room IDs. Completion and best-score IDs are unchanged.

## Difficulty progression

The previous order opened with a 160-move room and ended with a 90-move room. The new order introduces shorter plans first and ends with longer return trips and shared-object dependencies. Plan steps are manually authored explanations of distinct tasks, not an automated difficulty score. Their wording is not used to determine solvability.

| Band | Rooms | Mean shortened winning route | Mean plan steps |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Read the room | 5 | 81.6 | 3.4 |
| Set things up | 5 | 107.8 | 4.2 |
| Keep options open | 5 | 123.4 | 5 |
| The long game | 5 | 164.4 | 6.8 |

Difficulty is not strictly monotonic. “The travelling prop” is deliberately short but requires five connected role changes; water navigation in “Reed Channels” is less demanding conceptually than several shorter rooms. Move count measures execution length, and prose length depends on editorial choices. Blind human playtesting is still needed to establish how hard each room feels. We have strengthened the **ordering**, not claimed to prove that every later map is harder or added artificial walking.
